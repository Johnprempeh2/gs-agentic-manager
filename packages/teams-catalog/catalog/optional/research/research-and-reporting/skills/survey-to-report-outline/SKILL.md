# Survey to Report Outline

Use this skill when a batch of survey or interview data is ready and a report needs planning.

## Inputs

- The brief: who the report is for and the decisions it should help them make.
- The questionnaire or interview guide.
- The data: a survey export (CSV or spreadsheet), or interview notes and transcripts.
- The brand template, if the report goes outside the organisation.

## Steps

1. Write the purpose in one sentence: who reads this, and what they should decide after reading it.
2. Check the data. Count responses against the target, and note missing answers, duplicates and any group with fewer than 30 responses.
3. List three to five key questions the report must answer. Map each survey question or interview theme to one key question. Leave out anything that maps to none.
4. For each key question, name the tables or cross tabs needed, the open answers to code, and the sector benchmark to compare against, with its source.
5. Put the sections in order so the most important finding comes first.
6. Note the gaps: what the data cannot answer, and what the field team could collect next time.

## Output

A report outline with:

- Title and one-sentence purpose
- Executive summary placeholder (one page at most)
- Sections, each with the key question, the tables needed, the themes to code, and the benchmark and its source
- Method note: how people collected the data, where, when, and the sample size
- Gaps and caveats
- Next steps for the Data Analyst and the Report Writer
